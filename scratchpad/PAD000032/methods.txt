METHODS
This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the School of Public Health, Fudan University, China (IRB#2015-06-0553, IRB#2017-07-0629, IRB#2017-07-0629-S, IRB#2024-11-1179). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. No monetary compensation was provided to participants; however, all participants received free health examinations as part of the cohort study protocol. We selected 135 PWH who had experienced a stroke (HIV+/Stroke+) from the Comparative HIV and Aging Research in Taizhou cohort, which was described earlier. Matching was conducted in two sequential steps using the MatchIt package in R. First, each HIV+/Stroke+ individual was matched 1:1 to an HIV-positive, stroke-free individual (HIV+/Stroke−) by age and sex using nearest-neighbor matching without replacement; this comparison was designed to identify stroke-associated proteomic signatures within the HIV-infected population, with HIV status held constant to isolate stroke-specific effects. Second, each HIV+/Stroke− individual was subsequently matched 1:1 to an HIV-negative, stroke-free control (HIV−/Stroke−) enrolled in the same cohort using identical criteria; this comparison was designed to characterize the independent contribution of HIV infection to the circulating proteomic profile, independent of stroke. No age caliper was imposed in either step, as our primary objective was to retain all available stroke cases (n = 135) to ensure adequate statistical power. A statistically significant difference in continuous age remained after matching; continuous age was therefore included as an obligatory a priori covariate in all subsequent multivariable models.
For cross-sectional proteomic analysis, blood samples collected closest to the date of stroke occurrence were used for the HIV+/Stroke+ group, as no standardized enrollment timepoint existed for stroke cases; samples collected at the baseline enrollment were used for both control groups. For longitudinal proteomic analysis, 37 individuals in the HIV+/Stroke+ group who had one or more stored blood samples collected after HIV confirmation but prior to stroke onset were included (18 with one sample and 19 with two samples); these represented all individuals within the cohort for whom paired pre- and post-stroke proteomic data were available. As this sub-cohort is nested within the HIV+/Stroke+ group by design, the longitudinal analysis serves as a within-individual, self-controlled validation of the cross-sectional findings, with each participant serving as their own control across the pre- and post-stroke timepoints; this paired design inherently controls for stable between-individual confounders and provides greater statistical power per participant than independent cross-sectional comparisons,. Three age- and sex-matched groups of 135 individuals each were enrolled: people with HIV who had documented stroke (HIV+/Stroke+), people with HIV without stroke (HIV+/Stroke−), and HIV-negative individuals without stroke (HIV−/Stroke−). Plasma from all participants was profiled with the Olink Cardiovascular II and III panels (184 unique proteins) and used in four analyses. Analysis 1 identified stroke-related proteins through differential expression between HIV+/Stroke+ and HIV+/Stroke−, followed by KEGG pathway enrichment and protein–protein interaction analyses. Analysis 2 applied weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) among PWH to distinguish stroke-related from HIV-related signatures. Analysis 3 separately compared ischemic (n = 116) and hemorrhagic (n = 19) cases with newly age- and sex-matched HIV+/Stroke− controls at 1:1 and 1:4 ratios, respectively. Analysis 4 used generalized estimating equation models in 37 HIV+/Stroke+ individuals (18 with one pre-stroke sample, 19 with two) to test within-individual changes from pre-stroke to post-stroke timepoints.

Data were collected through standardized face-to-face interviews using structured questionnaires, and physical examinations were performed by trained health professionals. Biological sex, determined via self-report during standardized face-to-face questionnaire interviews, was explicitly considered in the study design as both a matching variable for control selection and a covariate in all multivariable analyses. The study population was predominantly male (82.2% in the HIV+/Stroke+ group). Due to the limited number of female participants, sex-stratified proteomic analyses were not performed. BMI was calculated as weight (kg) divided by height squared (m2). Fasting blood samples were collected during the survey and were immediately stored at −80 °C. Serum creatinine (SCR), TG, TC, HB, GLU, ALT, AST, and TBIL were measured using a Beckman Coulter automated biochemical analyzer.
HIV-related clinical information, including mode of transmission, time since diagnosis, ART regimens, and CD4 + T cell counts, was retrieved from the Chinese National HIV/AIDS Case Reporting and Information Management System.
Stroke diagnosis, including subtype classification and date of onset, was ascertained from the local Center for Disease Control and Prevention chronic disease registration and management system, which captures stroke events diagnosed at designated hospitals as part of China’s national chronic disease monitoring program. All included stroke cases were required to have a formal diagnosis certificate or neuroimaging documentation issued by a secondary or higher-level medical institution (hospital level II or above). To ensure diagnostic validity, the medical records of all included stroke cases were clinically adjudicated by qualified neurologists, requiring definitive neuroimaging confirmation (CT or MRI) of the acute event and its subtype, in accordance with the Chinese diagnostic criteria for cerebrovascular diseases and consistent with the World Health Organization definition of stroke. Cases lacking definitive neuroimaging evidence were excluded. This structured registry, combined with institutional certification requirements and neuroimaging-based adjudication, established a standardized and clinically verified basis for case ascertainment.

Plasma samples were collected in EDTA tubes, stored at −80 °C without prior thawing, and subjected to proteomic analysis. To maximize analytical coverage, all samples were analyzed simultaneously using both the Olink Target 96 Cardiovascular II and Cardiovascular III panels. Because these two panels consist of entirely distinct, non-overlapping sets of 92 target proteins, using two different panels inherently does not introduce inter-panel batch effects for any individual protein abundance measurement. This platform enables the comprehensive quantification of 184 unique cardiovascular-related protein biomarkers. Each target protein was recognized by a pair of antibodies conjugated with unique DNA oligonucleotide barcodes, followed by quantification using the Biomark HD system (Fluidigm, South San Francisco, CA), a high-throughput microfluidic real-time PCR instrument.
Rigorous quality control (QC) procedures were applied at both the plate and individual sample levels. Samples were distributed across 6 assay plates per panel. Each sample included four internal controls (two incubation controls, one extension control, and one detection control) to accurately monitor the immunoreaction, extension, and amplification/detection steps within each specific well. Each plate also contained dedicated external controls in separate wells: three inter-plate controls, three negative controls for background noise monitoring and limit of detection (LOD) calculation, and duplicated sample controls (pooled plasma) for variance assessment. Sample plates were evaluated based on the standard deviation of internal controls (< 0.2 NPX), and individual sample quality was assessed by evaluating deviations from the median control values (<0.3 NPX). Based on the dedicated sample controls, overall intra-assay coefficients of variation (CV) were 4% for both panels (reference threshold: <15%), and inter-assay CVs were 11% and 10% for Cardiovascular II and III, respectively (reference threshold: <25%), demonstrating satisfactory technical reproducibility. For the longitudinal sub-cohort, paired pre-stroke and post-stroke samples from the same individual were assigned to the same analytical plate to fundamentally eliminate plate-to-plate batch effects for within-subject comparisons.
Protein expression levels were reported as normalized protein expression (NPX) values, which are log2-scaled and relative measures, not reflecting absolute concentrations. Higher NPX values correspond to higher protein abundance. Values below the LOD were retained for downstream analyses in accordance with Olink’s official guidelines, which indicate that inclusion of sub-LOD values does not inflate false-positive discovery rates; the proportion of sub-LOD data points per protein is deposited in the Proteomics Identifications Database (PRIDE). Furthermore, to eliminate potential intra-panel batch effects across the 6 different assay plates, raw data were processed using Olink’s Intensity Normalization (v.2) method. Briefly, intra-plate technical variations were adjusted using built-in internal extension controls, and inter-plate variations were normalized by aligning the plate-specific median of each assay to the overall global median. The full quantification matrix containing NPX values, LOD thresholds, and individual sample QC results is also deposited in PRIDE (see “Data Availability”).

Baseline non-proteomic characteristics were compared across three groups: HIV+/Stroke+, HIV+/Stroke−, and HIV−/Stroke−. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. For continuous variables, those with a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using independent sample t-tests; non-normally distributed variables were presented as median with interquartile range (IQR) and analyzed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Differential expression analysis was conducted cross-sectionally between the HIV+/Stroke+ and HIV+/Stroke− groups. Prior to screening for differentially expressed proteins, we rigorously controlled for non-proteomic covariates. Candidate covariates were first screened from sociodemographic, biochemical, HIV-related, and comorbidity variables using univariate analysis; those with P < 0.05 were subsequently entered into a multivariable model. Variables that remained independently significant were retained, alongside established a priori stroke risk factors, as the final covariate set. Each protein was evaluated using the following multivariable logistic regression model: logit(P(Stroke = 1)) = β0 + β1(Protein NPX) + Σβk(Covariatesk), where Covariatesk includes age, sex, hypertension, and renal impairment (included a priori as established stroke risk factors irrespective of univariate significance), and time since HIV diagnosis, TG, ALT, AST, TBIL, BMI, and virological suppression (retained following univariate screening). Significance was defined as a false discovery rate (FDR)-adjusted P < 0.05. Because Olink NPX values are inherently log2-scaled, the log2 fold change (log2FC) was calculated directly as the mean NPX difference between groups (log2FC = Mean NPXHIV+/Stroke+–Mean NPXHIV+/Stroke−), yielding fold change magnitudes consistent with those reported in comparable Olink-based proteomics studies.
Because circulating inflammatory biomarkers often operate in highly co-regulated networks, conventional univariate approaches may fail to capture the multivariate structure of stroke-associated proteomic signatures in the presence of high inter-protein collinearity. We therefore concurrently applied orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), a supervised multivariate method widely used in metabolomic and proteomic biomarker discovery to address inherent multicollinearity. This multivariate technique robustly isolates predictive variation associated with stroke from orthogonal, non-predictive systemic noise, enabling the extraction of true biological signatures amidst the high heterogeneity of PWH. Features with a variable importance in projection (VIP) score > 1 were considered meaningful contributors to group separation. To maximize analytical stringency and minimize false positives, proteins were classified as significantly differentially expressed only if they met both the independent statistical criterion (FDR < 0.05) and the multivariate discriminative threshold (VIP > 1). This dual criterion ensures that reported proteins are statistically significant at the individual level and contribute meaningfully to the overall multivariate separation, reducing the risk of identifying proteins driven by correlated noise rather than true biological signal (Supplementary Fig.).
To evaluate discriminative performance, tenfold cross-validated ROC analysis compared three models: the proteomic signature, a combined model, and a basic clinical model. To reflect practical utility rather than exhaustive biological confounding, the clinical model only included readily accessible, routinely collected baseline factors (age, sex, BMI, hypertension, and renal impairment). CV-AUCs were compared using DeLong’s test with Bonferroni correction.

To explore the functional connectivity of the differentially expressed proteins, we constructed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network using the STRING database (v12.0),. To ensure high reliability, network edges were strictly limited to interactions supported by experimentally determined evidence, curated databases, and protein homology, applying a minimum required interaction score of 0.400. Functional clusters were identified using the Markov Cluster (MCL) algorithm based on stochastic flow simulation, with an inflation parameter of 3.
For functional evaluation, we performed KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. To prevent the analytical bias inherent to targeted proteomics, the enrichment background was restricted exclusively to the 184 unique proteins measured by the two Olink panels, rather than the entire human proteome. This custom background ensures that the enriched pathways reflect true disease-driven biological differences rather than the predefined cardiovascular focus of the assay. Pathways with FDR < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

To examine how the differentially expressed proteins relate to each other, we evaluated their localized expression patterns across the HIV+/Stroke+, HIV+/Stroke−, and HIV−/Stroke− groups, utilizing boxplots to visually compare their relative expression levels and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests to evaluate statistical significance. We calculated Spearman correlation coefficients specifically among these 16 significant proteins to assess their direct pairwise relationships. Hierarchical clustering based on Spearman correlation distance and the Ward.D2 agglomeration method was then applied to group these proteins into distinct clusters sharing similar expression trends.

In a separate analysis aimed at the global proteomic profile, we applied WGCNA to the complete dataset of all 184 quantified proteins to identify broader co-expression modules associated with stroke risk. We first constructed a signed network by calculating pairwise correlations across all 184 proteins. The resulting adjacency matrix was raised to a soft-thresholding power to emphasize strong connections and penalize weak ones. This was then transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to quantify network interconnectedness. Modules of co-expressed proteins were identified through hierarchical clustering of TOM-based dissimilarity.
To evaluate the association between these global protein modules and stroke, the module eigengene (the first principal component summarizing the expression profile of a given module) was extracted. Both univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to determine the OR and 95% confidence interval for each module eigengene with respect to stroke status. Multivariable models were adjusted for the identical set of covariates previously described in the differential expression analysis. Highly interconnected modules were exported for visualization in Cytoscape. In these network diagrams, connection strength (edge weight) between two proteins represents their pairwise TOM value, which captures topological overlap and shared network neighborhood within the module. To highlight the most robust co-expression relationships and filter out weak associations, a minimum TOM threshold of 0.02 was applied for edge visualization.

To account for the distinct pathophysiological mechanisms underlying different stroke etiologies, subgroup analyses were conducted separately for ischemic (HIV+/Ischemic stroke+) and hemorrhagic stroke (HIV+/Hemorrhagic stroke+) cases. Due to the reduced sample sizes in these subgroups, the threshold for differential protein expression was adapted to adjusted P < 0.05 and VIP > 1 to maintain discriminative robustness. Covariates were independently re-evaluated for each subtype using the same screening procedure described above (Supplementary Fig.). In this analysis, 116 HIV+/Ischemic stroke+ participants were 1:1 matched by age and sex with HIV+/Stroke− controls, while 19 HIV+/Hemorrhagic stroke+ participants were age- and sex-matched at 1:4 with HIV+/Stroke− controls to maximize statistical power.
To validate the cross-sectional proteomic findings, longitudinal self-controlled analyses were conducted using 37 individuals nested within the HIV+/Stroke+ group who had paired plasma samples before and after stroke onset. Generalized estimating equation models with a binomial distribution and logit link function were used to assess within-individual changes in protein expression across the pre- and post-stroke timepoints, with stroke occurrence as the binary outcome. An exchangeable working correlation structure was specified to accommodate the unbalanced repeated-measures design, in which participants contributed either two or three longitudinal observations, and robust sandwich standard errors were used to ensure valid inference. To adjust for variable sampling intervals, natural cubic splines were introduced to model the time variable flexibly. A significance threshold of P < 0.05 was applied. An identical analysis was performed in the subset of 29 HIV+/Ischemic stroke+ individuals with longitudinally paired samples to examine subtype-specific temporal protein dynamics.
