# PXD012704

Status: BLOCKED — no TMT 11-plex channel-to-sample map exists in any reachable source.

**Dataset:** "Alginate encapsulation of differentiating hiPSCs" — Chera lab, Department of Clinical
Science, University of Bergen, Norway (submitter Heidrun Vethe). Paper: Legøy TA, Vethe H, … Paulo JA,
Ræder H, Ghila L, Chera S. *Encapsulation boosts islet-cell signature in differentiating human induced
pluripotent stem cells via integrin signalling.* Sci Rep 2020;10:414. PMID 31942009, PMC6962451,
doi:10.1038/s41598-019-57305-x.

Step 0.5: no existing annotation upstream (`bigbio/sdrf-annotated-datasets` `datasets/PXD012704` and
`sandbox/PXD012704` both HTTP 404).

## Exact blocker

The deposit is one TMT 11-plex (all 11 channels used). Each of the 24 RAW files therefore needs 11
rows, each assigned to a specific sample. **Nothing that could be reached states which TMT channel
(126 … 131C) carries which sample.** Writing an SDRF would mean inventing the channel identities,
which the annotation rules forbid.

## What was established

- **Design (paper, Results + Fig. 2b/2c, "n = 2, 2, 2, 2, 3"):** 11 samples in one plex. There are 2 ×
  S5 cells on Matrigel (2D), 2 × S7 cells on Matrigel, 2 × S5bead[S0–S5] (alginate-encapsulated
  from iPSC stage to stage 5), and 2 × S7bead[S5–S7] (encapsulated from stage 5 to stage 7). These 8
  are from one healthy-donor hiPSC line (episomal reprogramming of skin fibroblasts). The last 3 are
  technical splits of one pooled human-islet mix (200 islets each from 4 deceased donors, 1 male and
  3 female, age 52–63, combined, then 15 µg split into three samples). The islet samples would be
  `pooled`, and the sex/age of the pool would be `not applicable` / `not available`.
- **Files (PRIDE `/files`, all 48 entries checked to belong to PXD012704):** 24 Thermo RAW files
  plus 24 Sequest mzIdentML files, one per RAW. There are no quantification tables, sample sheets
  or other files.
- **RAW headers (HTTP range read of all 24 files):**
  - The instrument is an **Orbitrap Fusion Lumos** (MS:1002732). PRIDE lists "Orbitrap Fusion"
    (MS:1002416), which is wrong. The LC is a Proxeon EASY-nLC.
  - The method is `…10plexTMT_MS3…` (SPS-MS3).
  - Sample IDs:
    - a11462–a11473 = `HV_11_01` … `HV_11_12` (150-min method, Oct 2018)
    - a11496–a11507 = `HV_11_01b` … `HV_11_12b` (120-min method, Oct 30 2018)
  - So there is one plex ("HV_11"), with 12 basic-pH reversed-phase fractions, each measured in two
    series. I could not tell whether the "b" series is a re-injection of the same 12 fractions or a
    second set of fractions. Peptide overlap between HV_11_01 and HV_11_01b is about 3× higher than
    between HV_11_01 and HV_11_02, but low in absolute terms.
  - The RAW sample-info fields hold no channel information: the comment field is a generic
    `Sample048`, and Study/Client/Laboratory are empty.
- **Search settings (mzIdentML `AnalysisProtocolCollection`):**
  - Fixed: TMT6plex/TMT 229.162932 on K and peptide N-term (UNIMOD:737), and Carbamidomethyl C
    (UNIMOD:4).
  - Variable: Oxidation M (UNIMOD:35).
  - Enzyme: Trypsin (KR), up to 2 missed cleavages.
  - Tolerances: precursor 50 ppm, fragment 1 Da (the PRIDE protocol says 0.9 Da).
  - Database: UniProt human 2014-02.

## Channel-map ladder (references/channel-map.md), every rung checked

1. **Paper and its supplementary** (Europe PMC full text of PMC6962451; MOESM1 PDF with 8 pages of
   supplementary figure legends and images; MOESM2–5 xlsx; Fig. 1–5): no per-channel table.
   - The xlsx files are gene-symbol lists of differentially expressed proteins.
   - Fig. 2b shows the five groups without channels.
   - The Fig. 2c heatmap column order comes from hierarchical clustering, so it is not a channel
     order.
   - Supp. Fig. 3a shows only group mean ± SEM TMT ratios.
2. **Deposited PRIDE files:** the mzIdentML files are identification-only, with no reporter-ion
   values and no sample labels. The RAW headers carry only fraction IDs (see above).
3. **Europe PMC supplementary** (`/PMC6962451/supplementaryFiles`): same files as rung 1, no map.
4. **External repositories in the Data Availability statement:** only GEO GSE141891 and GSE141309,
   both RNA-seq ("Expression profiling by high throughput sequencing"), with no proteomics layout.
   - The only other article citing PXD012704 is Legøy et al., Front Cell Dev Biol 2020 (PMID
     32161757, PMC7052484). It reuses these in-vitro and islet samples but gives no channel table.
     Its main text, Data_Sheet_1.PDF and Table_1–3.XLSX were checked; Supp. Fig. legend reads
     "n=2,2,2,3,5" over two plexes, again with no channels.
5. **Cross-validation:** there is no reconstructed map to cross-check.

Inferring the channels from reporter-ion intensity patterns (for example, which channels look most
islet-like) would be inference, not evidence, so it was not done.

## What would unblock it

Any one of these would unblock the dataset:

- A channel-to-sample table from the submitters (Heidrun Vethe, heidrun.vethe@uib.no, or Simona Chera,
  Simona.Chera@uib.no) or from the MS facility (Joao A. Paulo, Harvard Medical School), mapping
  TMT126–TMT131C to S5 ×2, S7 ×2, S5bead[S0–S5] ×2, S7bead[S5–S7] ×2 and islet pool ×3.
- A deposited protein/peptide quantification table with per-channel column headers named by sample.

With that table and a note on whether the `…b` series are re-injections (technical replicate 2 of
fractions 1–12) or separate fractions, the SDRF can be built directly. It would use 264 rows (24
files × 11 channels), with templates `ms-proteomics` + `human` (+ `cell-lines` for the hiPSC rows),
`factor value[treatment]` (2D Matrigel vs alginate encapsulation, by stage) and the technical values
listed above.
