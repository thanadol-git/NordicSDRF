## Templates

- `ms-proteomics v1.1.0`: label-free DDA LC-MS/MS on a Thermo Q Exactive HF.
- `human v1.1.0`: human patient biopsies. This template adds `characteristics[individual]`, which links each tumour/normal pair.
- `oncology-metadata v1.0.0`: this is a cancer study (clear cell renal cell carcinoma, ccRCC) of tumour versus adjacent non-tumour tissue. The template adds only optional columns. `characteristics[tumor size]` is used. Tumour grade and stage are not (see Limitations).

The following templates do not apply:

- `dia-acquisition`: the RAW method is a Top12 DDA method.
- `cell-lines`: the samples are primary tissue. MeSH lists "Cell Line, Tumor", but neither the PRIDE protocol nor the file names indicate any cell-line runs.
- `clinical-metadata`: this is an observational cohort with no treatment arm. Its columns would add nothing the human and oncology templates do not already carry.

## Design and file mapping

The PRIDE description says: "From partial or full nephrectomies of 11 patients, paired core biopsies of ccRCC affected tissue and adjacent non-tumorous tissue were both sampled and subjected to proteomics analyses". The biopsies were FFPE (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded). Three 10 µm sections per biopsy were deparaffinised, lysed in SDS/DTT at 99 °C, digested by FASP (filter-aided sample preparation) and desalted on Oasis HLB. Each sample was run as a single unfractionated 180-min gradient injection.

The deposit has 24 RAW files named `20151130_KF_<patient><T|N>.raw`. They cover 12 patient numbers (18, 21, 22, 27, 29, 31, 33, 39, 44, 46, 48, 53), with T = tumour and N = adjacent normal. Every RAW header (first 64 KB, read by HTTP range request) records the original acquisition path `...\2015-11-30_QE-HF_Nyreprosjekt\Raw\<same file name>`, so the files were not renamed or swapped after acquisition. The remaining deposited files are not runs:

- 30 `progenesis-export_N.mgf` files. These are Progenesis feature exports pooled across runs, not per-run peak lists.
- One 4.36 GB PeptideShaker `.mzid` file.

Patient 48 is the exception: its rows are named `patient 48 tumor site biopsy` and `patient 48 adjacent normal site biopsy`, both with biological replicate 11.

- **Factor value:** `factor value[disease]` is `clear cell renal carcinoma` (MONDO:0005005) on the 11 T rows of the published patients and `normal` on their 11 N rows. It is `not available` on both patient-48 rows. The project-level disease was not broadcast onto the normal rows.
- **Biological replicate:** this numbers independent patients within each condition. The tumour and normal biopsies from one patient share the number and the `individual` value, so they are paired. Technical replicate and fraction are 1 throughout, because there is one unfractionated injection per biopsy.

**How the sample-to-file mapping was established.**

1. Condition and patient come directly from the deposited file names, which match the original acquisition paths in the RAW headers.
2. Landolt et al. 2017, Table 1 lists patients by the same numbers and has a "Sample usage" column, where P = proteomics. It marks exactly 18, 21, 22, 27, 29, 31, 33, 39, 44, 46 and 53 with P. These are 11 patients, matching the "11 patients" of the proteomics paper, and they are 11 of the 12 patient numbers in the file names.
3. Patient 48 is in none of the three published tables. That paper notes that two further patients were excluded for mixed carcinoma/normal biopsies, but it does not identify them.

Both patient-48 runs are kept because they are genuine deposited runs, and every deposited RAW file is annotated. Patient 48 is outside the published 11-patient analysis: PRIDE, the abstract and Landolt 2017 Table 1 all account for exactly 11 patients. It is likely one of the two patients Landolt 2017 Methods describe as excluded "because of a mixture of carcinoma and normal tissue in the respective biopsies", but this is unconfirmed.

The T/N suffix shows the intended biopsy site, not confirmed histology or tissue purity. So disease and factor value are `not available` on both rows, and so are age, sex, developmental stage and tumour size.

**Per-patient values.** Age and sex are copied from the published patient tables. Every patient listed in more than one table has identical age and sex in each. `adult` (EFO:0001272) is set from each patient's tabulated age (46–78 years). `tumor size` is taken from the "Size (mm)" column: Eikrem 2016 Table 1 for patients 18–33, and Strauss 2018 Table 1 for patients 39–53. It is `not applicable` on the normal rows of the 11 published patients and `not available` on both patient-48 rows.

## Technical values and their sources

- **Instrument**: `Q Exactive HF` (MS:1002523). All 24 RAW headers say "Q Exactive HF - Orbitrap MS", the protocol says Q-Exactive HF, and the sequence folder is named `..._QE-HF_...`. PRIDE's dropdown lists plain `Q Exactive` (MS:1001911), and the RAW header outranks it.
- **Acquisition**: DDA (`Data-dependent acquisition`, PRIDE:0000627). The protocol says "data-dependent-acquisition mode", top 12, and the RAW method is `Sensitive_TOP12HCD_120K-30K_2mmSH_IT2e4_195minRUN.meth`.
- **Dissociation, collision energy, analyser and scan range**: HCD, written as `beam-type collision-induced dissociation` (MS:1000422, the spec's canonical accession for HCD), `28 NCE`, MS2 in the orbitrap (MS:1000484, R = 30,000), and MS1 scan range `375m/z-1500m/z`. All are from the protocol, and the RAW method name (TOP12HCD, 120K–30K) agrees.
- **Cleavage agent**: `Trypsin` (MS:1001251).
  - The deposited mzid's `Enzyme` element is Trypsin with 2 missed cleavages, fully specific.
  - In two range-read chunks of the mzid's `Peptide` section, 93.9% of 488 peptides and 92.9% of 479 peptides end in K or R.
  - The PRIDE prose says "FASP" without naming the enzyme, which is why `sdrf-tools reconcile` raises `cleavage_agent_unsupported`. The deposited search output is the higher-precedence source, so the value is kept.
  - The cited FASP protocol (Wisniewski 2013) digests with Lys-C and then trypsin, so a Lys-C pre-digestion cannot be ruled out. Neither the PRIDE protocol nor the search names Lys-C, so it is not annotated.
- **Modifications**: read from the mzid's `ModificationParams`, located by binary-searching byte offsets with range reads. The whole file was never downloaded. There are six:
  - Carbamidomethyl, UNIMOD:4, C, fixed.
  - Oxidation, UNIMOD:35, M, variable.
  - Acetyl, UNIMOD:1, protein N-term, variable.
  - Glu->pyro-Glu, UNIMOD:27, E at peptide N-term, variable.
  - Gln->pyro-Glu, UNIMOD:28, Q at peptide N-term, variable.
  - Ammonia-loss, UNIMOD:385, C at peptide N-term, variable.
- **Tolerances**: 10 ppm precursor and 0.5 Da fragment. The mzid's `ParentTolerance`/`FragmentTolerance` and the data-processing protocol agree.
- **Reduction reagent**: `Dithiothreitol (DTT)` (PRIDE:0000608, label checked in OLS), from the lysis buffer (0.1 M DTT, 99 °C for 1 h).
- **Alkylation reagent**: `not available`. The fixed carbamidomethylation shows that alkylation was done, but no source names the reagent.
- **Label**: label free sample. The PRIDE quantification method is MS1 intensity-based label-free quantification.

## Limitations

- **Primary paper not read in full.** The Koch et al. 2019 full text was unavailable (HTTP 403 from the publisher, no PMC or repository copy; the NVA/BORA record has no file). Design facts come from its abstract, MeSH terms and the PRIDE protocols.
- **Patient numbering is cross-paper inference.** The link between file-name patient numbers and the published patient tables rests on the group's shared numbering. It is strongly supported: Landolt 2017 Table 1 marks exactly 11 of the 12 file patient numbers as used for proteomics, which matches the paper's "11 patients". But it was not confirmed in the proteomics paper itself. Age, sex, developmental stage and tumour size depend on this link.
- **Patient 48** (`20151130_KF_48T.raw`, `20151130_KF_48N.raw`) is deposited but appears in no published table and is outside the published 11-patient analysis. It is likely one of the two patients that Landolt 2017 excluded for mixed carcinoma/normal biopsies (unconfirmed). Its T/N file suffixes give only the intended biopsy site, not confirmed histology or purity. So `characteristics[disease]`, `factor value[disease]`, age, sex, developmental stage and tumour size are all `not available` on rows 11 and 23. Only the source and assay names carry the T/N distinction.
- **Tumour grade and stage are not annotated.** The group's papers disagree for some patients: patient 44 has Fuhrman grade 2 in Landolt 2017 but 4 in Strauss 2018, and patient 18 has stage III in Eikrem 2016 but cancer stage 2 in Landolt 2017. pTNM strings agree across papers but were left out rather than mapped to NCIT finding terms.
- **Tumour size has a single source per patient.** The papers with size columns cover disjoint patient sets, so no size value could be cross-checked.
- **Search-description mismatch.** The deposited mzid is a SearchGUI (X!Tandem)/PeptideShaker 1.2.0 search of the Progenesis-exported MGFs, against UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot human (20,197 entries, 2015-08-06, target-decoy). The PRIDE data-processing protocol instead describes Proteome Discoverer 1.4 with only carbamidomethyl (fixed) and oxidation (variable). The `comment[modification parameters]` columns follow the deposited PeptideShaker mzid (six modifications), not the Proteome Discoverer search described in the PRIDE protocol and the paper. Tolerances (10 ppm, 0.5 Da) and the enzyme agree between the two.
- **Alkylation reagent** is `not available`: it is implied by the fixed carbamidomethylation but never named.
- **Cell type and ancestry category** are `not available`. PRIDE's "Epithelial cell" dropdown is not a per-sample fact for whole-biopsy lysates. The patients were treated in Bergen, Norway, but no per-patient ancestry is reported.
- **Preservation not encoded.** The FFPE preservation of the biopsies has no dedicated column in the chosen templates. It is recorded here and in the manifest only.
