# PXD008467 annotation blocked

PXD008467 is the Odense University Hospital (Hans Christian Beck, Dept. Clinical Biochemistry and Pharmacology, Odense, Denmark) deposit titled "Plasma proteomics reveals the confounding effect of heparin administration on the analysis of candidate cardiovascular biomarkers - Cohort1". It is the discovery (controlled intervention) arm of Beck HC et al., Clin Chem 2018;64(10):1474-1484 (PMID 30115630, doi:10.1373/clinchem.2017.282665). No upstream annotation exists (`bigbio/sdrf-annotated-datasets` `datasets/PXD008467` and `sandbox/PXD008467` both HTTP 404).

## Blocker

This is a TMT10plex study, and no channel-to-sample map exists in any reachable source. Each of the 5 TMT sets carries 10 channels. Only one channel is identified: 126 is the pooled baseline (pre-heparin) sample of the 9 patients. The other 9 channels per set hold the individual baseline, 2 min, 15 min and 60 min post-heparin plasma samples of two patients, plus a second pool. Nothing in the record says which patient or time point is in which channel of which set. Without that, `characteristics[individual]`, the time point and `factor value[...]` cannot be assigned to 45 of the 50 (set × channel) rows. Per the non-interactive rules, channel identities must not be invented, so no SDRF is emitted.

The record is also internally inconsistent about the layout. The PRIDE sample-processing protocol says "eight individual labeled samples and two appropriate pools" per run, but lists only 7 non-126 tags (127N, 127C, 128N, 128C, 129N, 130N, 130C; 129C and 131 are not mentioned). The deposited Proteome Discoverer study quantifies all 10 channels. Also, 9 patients × 4 time points = 36 individual samples, while 5 sets × 8 slots = 40. So even a generic "patient A in channels x-y, patient B in z-w" layout cannot be derived from the protocol text.

## What was established

- **Organism / material:** Homo sapiens blood plasma, enriched on agarose-linked para-aminobenzamidine (ABA) beads. The samples come from 9 patients at intravascular-ultrasound follow-up 12 months after acute myocardial infarction. Each patient was sampled before heparin and 2, 15 and 60 min after (abstract and PRIDE protocol).
- **Digestion / labelling:** on-bead reduction with DTT and alkylation with iodoacetamide, then trypsin (50:1, 37 °C overnight) and TMT10plex labelling. The peptides were fractionated (fractionation method described only as "as described").
- **Search (PRIDE data-processing protocol):** Proteome Discoverer 2.1.0.81 with Mascot and SEQUEST. Settings: precursor 8 ppm, fragment 0.05 Da (HCD), 2 missed cleavages, fixed Carbamidomethyl (C) and TMT on N-termini/K (the protocol says "6-plex TMT", which is the same UNIMOD:737 mass), variable Oxidation (M) and Deamidated (N). Database: SwissProt human.
- **Instrument:** Q Exactive, confirmed from the RAW header of `ABA_PILOT_1_BA10_1.raw`. The header gives the instrument as "Q Exactive - Orbitrap MS", the method as `70min_HCD30_iTRAQ_res70000_12_15_30.meth` (HCD), and the original acquisition name as `ABA_PILOT_1_BA10_161204221929.raw`. The PRIDE instrument field matches.
- **Files:** 305 RAW files plus one search result, `ABA_PILOT_FINAL.pdResult` (32.8 GB). Every PRIDE v3 `files/all` entry carries `projectAccessions` = PXD008467. The file prefixes `ABA_PILOT_1` to `ABA_PILOT_5` are the 5 TMT sets, with 62 / 62 / 62 / 57 / 62 files. Each set was run as 31 autosampler positions (fractions) in two LC-MS series, Dec 2016 and Jan 2017. The second injections carry `_2`, or `_1`/`_2` in set 1. The deposited `ABA_PILOT_4_*` has 57 files, while the PD study lists 56 for that set.
- **Deposited search result (pdResult):** read via HTTP range requests through an SQLite VFS, not downloaded. In table `AnalysisDefinition`, the `StudyDefinition` "ABA_PILOT_200217" has `<Factors />` (no study factors) and 5 FileSets (`ABA_PILOT_1_B`, `ABA_PILOT_2_B`, `ABA_PILOT_3_B`, `ABA_PILOT_4_GB`, `ABA_PILOT_5_B`). Its 50 samples all have the generic Proteome Discoverer default names `ABA_PILOT_n_X - [channel]` with empty `<FactorValues />`. Channel 126 is `SampleType="Control"` in every set and the other 9 channels are `"Sample"`. That is the generic default, not a map.

## Channel-map ladder (references/channel-map.md), all rungs checked

1. **Paper and supplementary:** not open access. Europe PMC lists no PMCID and `hasSuppl=N`; Unpaywall and OpenAlex report it as closed. The OUP landing page, PDF (`article-pdf/64/10/1474/32642945/clinchem1474.pdf`) and supplementary URLs all return HTTP 403 (Cloudflare challenge). The SDU and KU (University of Copenhagen) research-portal records hold no full text. Only the abstract and MeSH terms could be used, and neither contains a channel layout.
2. **Deposited PRIDE files:** only RAW files and the pdResult. The pdResult study design has generic default sample names only (see above). There is no sample sheet.
3. **Europe PMC supplementary:** not applicable, because the paper is not in PMC.
4. **External repositories in Data Availability:** none found. The Data Availability statement could not be read (paywall), and PubMed efetch lists no databank links.
5. **Cross-validation:** not possible without a candidate map.

## What would unblock it

Any of the following would allow a complete, honest SDRF:

- the paper's supplementary material, or a table from the authors, giving patient × time point per (TMT set, channel), including what the second pool is and which channel it occupies;
- a Proteome Discoverer `.pdStudy` or study export with sample annotations.

The build could then use one source per patient × time point plus the pools, with `factor value[time]` (0/2/15/60 min after heparin). All 305 RAW files would map to 31 fractions × 2 injections per set (technical replicates 1 and 2). The second injections of set 4 need checking (57 deposited files versus 56 in the PD study).
